# Plan your page

Plan a useful link-in-bio or one-page website: choose its purpose, arrange the content and follow the guides for design, publishing and measurement.

## Give the page one clear responsibility

People arrive from a social profile, a QR code, an email signature or a search result with little context. Help them recognize you, understand what matters and complete a useful action.

Write down the primary outcome before choosing a theme. A consultant may want visitors to book a call. A venue may need the next event and directions. A creator may lead with a new release. A personal page may bring interests and contact links together. This decision determines the order of the page.

Good structure answers three questions quickly: who is this, what is current, and what can I do here?

## Arrange the content in a useful order

1. **Identity.** Use a recognizable name, image or logo and one sentence that explains the page.
2. **Primary action.** Put the most important destination first and name the action. "Book a table" tells visitors more than "Click here".
3. **Current or featured content.** Highlight the release, event, offer or announcement that deserves attention. Remove it when it expires.
4. **Supporting destinations.** Group related links and give them distinct labels. Avoid repeated variants of the same action.
5. **Contact and trust.** Include social profiles, contact details, location or legal information when those details help visitors decide.

## Decide how much belongs on the page

There is no universal number of links. Include enough options for the visitor's likely tasks, then use order and grouping to make the important action easy to find. Giving every destination equal prominence makes the page harder to scan.

A focused page can replace a small brochure website when the goal is identity, selected content and contact. Deep documentation, a large catalog or content that depends on complex navigation needs a more extensive structure. Use additional pages for subjects that deserve their own URL.
