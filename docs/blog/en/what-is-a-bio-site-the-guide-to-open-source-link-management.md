**A bio site is a short, mobile-friendly web page that gathers your important links and information behind one URL.** Put that URL in a social profile, email signature or QR code so visitors can choose what to do next: see your work, read a menu, book a call or visit your shop.

The term describes a type of page. [Bio Sites](<https://biosites.com/>) is also the name of a specific link-in-bio service. If you searched for “what is bio.site?”, that address leads to the Bio Sites service. This guide explains the general idea, shows working examples and explains how to create a page with OrbitPage.

## What does a bio site look like?

A useful bio site introduces its owner and gives visitors a small number of clear next steps. It can contain more than buttons: a short description, images, contact details, a menu or product information can help someone decide before leaving the page.

<a id="bio-site-vs-website"></a>

## How is a bio site different from a full website?

| Question | Bio site | Larger website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main purpose | Help someone choose their next action from one compact page. | Explain several subjects through separate pages and navigation. |
| Typical content | A short introduction, selected links and essential details. | Service pages, articles, documentation, catalogues and other detailed material. |
| When it fits | You need one address for a social profile, campaign or QR code. | Visitors need substantial information about several different topics. |

You can use both. A compact page can direct visitors to articles, booking tools or a larger website. It does not have to replace them.

<a id="what-to-include"></a>

## What should you put on a bio site?

1.  **A recognisable name and introduction.** Explain who the page belongs to and what visitors can find.
2.  **One clear priority.** Put the action that matters most near the top, such as “View the menu” or “Book a consultation”.
3.  **Specific link labels.** “Watch the latest tutorial” gives more information than several buttons called “Click here”.
4.  **Useful supporting details.** Add contact information, an image or a short explanation when it helps someone choose.
5.  **Current destinations.** Remove expired offers and check links on a phone before sharing the page.

A restaurant might put its menu, booking link and location first. A photographer might lead with a portfolio, availability and enquiries. These are possible arrangements; the same builder can serve other purposes.

<a id="measure-a-bio-site"></a>

## How do you know whether the page works?

Decide what success means before adding more links. Visits tell you that the page was opened. Link clicks tell you that someone selected a destination. Neither number alone proves a booking, purchase or subscription.

Use consistent campaign labels where appropriate and confirm the final result in the destination system. The [link click tracking guide](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/link-click-tracking-how-to-monitor-and-measure-engagement>) explains how to distinguish these measures.

<a id="common-questions"></a>

## Common questions

### Is a bio site only for Instagram?

No. You can share the same public address on other social platforms, in an email signature, on printed material or through a QR code.

### Can a bio site appear in search?

An accessible, indexable public page can be considered by search engines. A useful title, descriptive text and working links help explain it, but publication does not guarantee indexing or rankings.

### Do I need a custom domain?

You can start with the public address supplied by your hosting service. A custom domain can make the address easier to recognise; availability and configuration depend on the service or your own hosting setup.

### How many links should I include?

Include the destinations that help your visitors. Start with a short, prioritised list and add more only when they serve a clear purpose. A longer list is not automatically more useful.
