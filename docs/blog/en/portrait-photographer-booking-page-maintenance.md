# Keep a Portrait Photographer’s Booking Page Current

A practical routine for maintaining portrait-session links, retiring stale offers, assigning page ownership, and checking the live enquiry route.

A portrait page can look polished while quietly sending visitors to an old booking form or promoting sessions that have ended. A simple maintenance routine keeps the offer, page, and destination aligned without requiring a full redesign.

<a id="section-1"></a>

## Map the page and its destinations

Give every link a clear visitor task: view selected portraits, understand your sessions, ask a question, or request a booking. Use labels that explain the action, such as “Ask about a portrait session,” rather than a vague “Contact.” Remove links that no longer help someone take a relevant next step.

Record where each piece of information lives and who can change it. You control the wording and links on your public page, while a booking calendar or enquiry form may be managed in a separate service. Updating the page does not update those external destinations automatically.

Name a maintainer, even if that person is you, and keep a brief handover note with the destinations and their owners. OrbitPage lets you edit page details and organize content blocks; deciding when to review them and checking external services are manual responsibilities.

<a id="section-2"></a>

## Build a review routine that fits your work

Set a recurring reminder based on how often your availability, session locations, or offers change. Review sooner whenever one of those details changes. Open the published page as a visitor, not only the editing view, and follow the important routes through to their destinations.

On a phone-sized screen, check whether a visitor can quickly identify your portrait work and find the intended next step. Confirm that selected images load and that captions still describe the work accurately. These are checks you perform yourself; the page editor does not confirm that an external form or booking calendar is current.

If you edit the browser title or search description in OrbitPage’s Page settings, compare them with the live page. Search engines and messaging apps may keep older previews for a while, so a saved change does not guarantee an immediate refresh everywhere.

-   Check whether any offer or session wording has an end date that has passed.
-   Confirm the named person who will handle the next page and destination review.
-   Make sure the handover note is stored privately if it includes account details.

## Hypothetical example: replace an expired spring offer

Hypothetical example: Mira, a fictional independent photographer, has a public page promoting a spring portrait offer. The offer has ended, but its button still opens a form titled “Spring mini-session request.” The mismatch could lead a visitor to believe those sessions are still available.

Mira removes the expired promotion and uses current, non-date-specific wording: “Portrait sessions are arranged by enquiry. Tell me the kind of session you have in mind, and I’ll reply with current availability.” She changes the button to “Ask about a portrait session” only after confirming that the replacement form is ready.

Because the form is external, Mira opens it separately and checks its title and questions for references to spring dates. She then visits the public page and follows the button to confirm that the route reaches the intended form. If she has not confirmed specific dates, she avoids suggesting that visitors can book them immediately.

There is a tradeoff: a general enquiry route can remain accurate longer, but visitors must ask before they know what availability looks like. A direct calendar makes the next step clearer when its dates and session options are kept up to date.

<a id="section-4"></a>

## Keep the next update manageable

Keep a short record of each important destination, what it is for, and who can change it. Mark which details appear on the public page and which belong to an external service. That small distinction helps prevent a page edit from leaving an outdated form or calendar untouched.

Before a substantial change, consider whether you need a backup. OrbitPage’s content-only export covers the main-page block list; it does not restore profile, theme, menu, subpages, privacy settings, accounts, or uploaded-file contents. A broader backup can include selected sections, and referenced media may need to be included separately. Store backups privately because they can contain unpublished content and configuration.

Now open your published page, tap its main portrait-session button, and confirm that the destination matches the sessions you currently offer.
