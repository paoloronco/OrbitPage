# A QR code campaign works only when the destination stays useful

Use QR codes on print, packaging and signs with a stable destination, a clear promise and a way to review campaign visits.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/qr-codes-for-offline-campaigns>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

A QR code is a physical promise: someone points a phone at a printed object and expects a useful page. Unlike a digital post, the print may stay in place long after the campaign changes. Plan the destination and maintenance before printing the square.

<a id="promise"></a>

## Tell people what scanning will open.

A poster can say 'View the next show and tickets'; packaging can say 'Setup guide and support.' The page should lead with that promised action instead of dropping visitors at a generic list of every social profile.

Use a dedicated page when the context is very different from the main profile. A table menu, product instructions and event booking are distinct tasks, even if they share one brand.

<a id="print"></a>

## Scan the printed code at its intended location.

Test the code at its intended size, distance and lighting before printing a batch. Check it with more than one phone and open the destination over a mobile connection. A code that works on a bright monitor can be difficult to use on curved packaging or a dim wall.

Keep a readable short URL beside the code so a person can type the address or share it without a camera. This also helps when the print is photographed rather than scanned.

<a id="address"></a>

## Keep the public address valid after the campaign ends.

Choose an address you can continue to operate. When a promotion ends, update the destination with a current offer or a clear explanation rather than leaving printed codes pointed at a 404 or an expired discount.

If you change domains later, test the old printed address and any redirect. Printed material cannot be patched as easily as a social caption.

<a id="measure"></a>

## Tag campaigns without confusing scans with outcomes.

A distinct campaign URL can help separate a poster, package or partner location in reports. Use consistent labels and compare visits with the action the print promised.

A visit does not prove someone purchased, booked or completed setup. Check those outcomes in the system that owns them, and avoid collecting more information than the decision requires.
