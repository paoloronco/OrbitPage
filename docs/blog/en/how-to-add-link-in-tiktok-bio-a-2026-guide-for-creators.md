## Bypass follower limits by switching to a Business Account

Accounts that have not yet reached the 1,000-follower threshold required for Personal Account links can access the website field immediately by converting to a Business Account. This transition removes the follower requirement, allowing new creators or small brands to direct traffic to external sites from day one. It is a strategic move for those prioritizing lead generation or sales over organic trend participation, as it bypasses the standard growth period usually necessary to unlock profile utility.

To initiate the switch, navigate to the profile page and tap the three-line menu icon in the top right corner. Select "Settings and privacy," tap "Account," and then choose "Switch to Business Account." The app will prompt the user to select a category that best describes their niche, such as "Electronics" or "Software & Apps," which helps the platform tailor content insights. Once the conversion is complete, the "Edit profile" menu will display a "Website" field that was previously hidden or inactive for accounts with low follower counts.

The primary compromise involved in this switch is the restriction of the music library. Business Accounts are limited to the Commercial Music Library, which consists of pre-cleared tracks for promotional use. Most mainstream, copyrighted songs available to Personal Accounts are restricted to prevent licensing violations by entities using the platform for profit. Creators must weigh the value of a clickable link against the ability to use trending audio, as the inability to use viral tracks can impact the reach of certain content formats.

Switching back to a Personal Account is possible through the same settings menu, though doing so will typically remove the website link if the account still lacks 1,000 followers. Before committing, users should review the current Business Account terms and feature sets on TikTok’s official support pages, as platform policies regarding commercial usage and link eligibility are subject to update. ## Troubleshoot common reasons the website field is missing

The most frequent error occurs when a user enters their URL into the "Bio" text box instead of the designated "Website" field. The Bio field is restricted to plain text and does not support active hyperlinking; any URL placed there will appear as unclickable text that followers cannot tap to follow. To enable a functional link, the account must access the specific Website field within the "Edit Profile" menu. If this field is absent, the account likely does not meet the current 2026 requirements for follower count or has not yet transitioned to a Business Account, which remains a prerequisite for external linking.

![A magnifying glass hovering over a website input field with a link icon, positioned below a text-filled bio field.](<https://assets.rankbench.io/storage/v1/object/public/calendar-media/3cb5b730-062d-4cb9-9bfc-32a3e101f3a3/sections/troubleshoot-common-reasons-the-website-field-is-missing-1791627923753.webp>)

App version lag and regional restrictions also prevent the Website field from appearing. Users should verify that the TikTok application is updated to the latest build through the App Store or Google Play Store, as outdated software often fails to render new interface elements or account features. Additionally, TikTok may restrict linking capabilities in specific geographic regions due to local digital advertising regulations or for accounts registered to users under the age of 18. Checking the account's registered birthdate and region settings in the privacy menu can clarify if these systemic blocks are in effect.

If a link is successfully added to the Website field but appears as plain text, the issue usually stems from a protocol error or a safety flag. Ensure the URL includes the full "https://" prefix, as the platform requires the complete protocol to recognize the string as a valid hyperlink. If the link remains inactive after correcting the prefix, the destination domain may be flagged by TikTok’s security filters. Utilizing a recognized landing page aggregator can often bypass these visibility issues, provided the aggregator's domain is in good standing with the platform's current safety protocols.
