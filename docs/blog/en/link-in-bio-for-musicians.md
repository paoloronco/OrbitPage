# A link-in-bio page for musicians: release first, everything else in order

Plan a music-release link-in-bio page that separates the current track, listening choices, live dates and press contact.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/link-in-bio-for-musicians>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

A music release asks several people to do different things. A fan wants to listen, a promoter may need dates, and a journalist may need a short biography and contact. One page can serve all three if the current release leads and the supporting routes stay easy to recognize.

<a id="release"></a>

## Give the new work one unmistakable destination.

Lead with the artist name, release title, artwork and a short explanation of why someone should open it. If listeners use several platforms, group those destinations under one clearly named release section instead of repeating the same song across the page.

Keep the public address stable between releases. Replace the featured work when the campaign changes, but retain an archive route for people who came looking for older material.

<a id="live"></a>

## Separate listening from tickets and venue information.

Show the next confirmed date, city and ticket destination. If an event has ended, remove its prominent card or move it into an archive. A ticket button with an old date weakens trust faster than an imperfect color choice.

For several shows, give fans a clear dates section and keep inventory in the ticketing service that owns it. Do not imply that a page card reflects remaining seats unless it actually does.

<a id="industry"></a>

## Make professional contact easy without crowding fans.

A short artist description, selected work and a separate booking or press email can be enough for first contact. The page should make it clear who handles each enquiry and which information a promoter can find elsewhere.

Put the press route after the fan actions unless professional enquiries are the page's primary purpose. A promoter will look for a descriptive label; a first-time listener should not have to navigate around a media kit.

<a id="review"></a>

## Check the handoff, then update the page for the next moment.

Test listening links in the apps your audience actually uses, and check tickets from a logged-out browser. A campaign link that opens the wrong version of a track wastes the attention you earned.

Review visits and action clicks by campaign, but judge the release in the music and ticket systems too. The page records a handoff, not every completed listen or sale.
