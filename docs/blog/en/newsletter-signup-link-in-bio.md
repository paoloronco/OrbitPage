# How to make newsletter signup useful on a link-in-bio page

Invite newsletter signups from a link-in-bio page with a clear promise, explicit consent and a useful first message.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/newsletter-signup-link-in-bio>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

A newsletter signup competes with every other action on a small public page. It earns space when people understand what they will receive and why they should trust the sender. A vague 'stay updated' button is weaker than a specific promise the owner can keep.

<a id="promise"></a>

## Describe the emails before asking for an address.

Tell visitors the topic, likely frequency and useful outcome. 'Monthly studio openings and new work' sets a clearer expectation than 'Join my list.' Place the invitation after the work or offer that makes it relevant.

A creator might promise early release notes; a venue might share the next program. Do not promise exclusive access or a fixed schedule unless you can maintain it.

<a id="consent"></a>

## Use a clear opt-in path and a working exit.

Ask only for the information needed to send the promised messages. OrbitPage's public newsletter flow requires explicit consent and confirms the address before activating a subscriber; eligible workspaces use their own SMTP provider for delivery.

Keep the sender identity and unsubscribe route clear. A page signup should not be used as permission for unrelated campaigns or a cold-contact import.

<a id="welcome"></a>

## Deliver the reason someone joined.

Send a useful first message that confirms the topic and points to one current resource, release or next step. Avoid making the first email a broad sales pitch if the page promised practical updates.

Test signup, confirmation and unsubscribe with an address you control. The workflow is only finished when the recipient can complete it outside the dashboard.

<a id="measure"></a>

## Compare signups with the quality of the audience.

A high signup count means little if messages go unopened or people leave immediately. Review aggregate delivery and engagement alongside the page's other goals, and adjust the promise if subscribers expected something else.

Keep signup secondary when booking or purchase is the visitor's urgent task. The best location depends on what the page is asking people to do today.
