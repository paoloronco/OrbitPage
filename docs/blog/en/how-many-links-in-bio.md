# How many links should you put in a link-in-bio page?

Decide how many links a link-in-bio page needs by grouping visitor tasks, removing duplicates and choosing one clear primary action.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/how-many-links-in-bio>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

There is no universal number of buttons that makes a link-in-bio page work. The useful limit is the number of decisions a visitor can understand without losing the reason they came. Count distinct jobs, not every platform account or asset you own.

<a id="job"></a>

## Choose the action the page should make easier today.

Write one sentence before adding links: 'This page helps a visitor book a table,' or 'This page helps a listener find the new release.' A page can support other routes, but one current task should set the order.

If you cannot name that task, removing buttons will not fix the problem. Talk to the people who use the page or inspect the questions they ask most often, then build around the answer.

<a id="groups"></a>

## Give supporting routes different roles.

Keep the main action prominent, then group proof, contact, social channels and older work where they help the decision. A portfolio, three social profiles and a booking link are not five equal choices: one may be the action and the others evidence or follow-up.

Use a compact social area for networks people expect to find. Give a full card only to a destination that needs a description or deserves attention now.

<a id="remove"></a>

## Cut links that lead to the same decision.

Two buttons for the same booking tool, several versions of an expired campaign and generic 'website' links often add scanning work without giving visitors a new option. Keep the clearest route and update old references when the destination changes.

Do not hide necessary information just to reach a tidy number. A venue may genuinely need menu, reservations, hours and directions; a creator may need release, dates and contact.

<a id="test"></a>

## Watch whether visitors find the promised route.

Open the page on a phone and ask someone unfamiliar with it to find the main action. If they hesitate, first revise the order and labels. Only then decide whether the number of visible links is the obstacle.

Review clicks alongside the final outcome. A rarely used safety, accessibility or contact route may still be worth keeping even when it is not the most popular button.
