# What should a local business put on its link-in-bio page?

Build a link-in-bio page for a local service business with clear location, hours, proof and a booking or enquiry route.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/link-in-bio-for-local-business>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

A local customer often arrives with a practical question: can this business help me, is it near me and how do I book? A public page should answer those questions before asking the visitor to browse a gallery of social channels. The right details vary by business, but the decision order is remarkably consistent.

<a id="identity"></a>

## Say what the business does and where it serves people.

A visitor from a social post may know the business name but not the service area. State the main service, city or neighborhood and whether the work happens at a location, at the customer's site or online. Keep the name and contact details consistent with the other public profiles you maintain.

For a barber this may be 'Haircuts in Bristol, by appointment.' For a repair service it may be the types of work and the towns covered. Use only locations and availability you can actually serve; a long list of nearby places is not a useful substitute for the area you serve.

<a id="action"></a>

## Match the button to how the business takes work.

If customers can reserve a slot, make booking the primary action. If the job needs a quote, use a short enquiry route and say what information to send. If customers usually call for urgent availability, make the phone action obvious and keep the number current.

A secondary action can answer the next most common question: services, menu, directions or opening hours. Avoid giving maps, every social network, a newsletter and the enquiry form identical visual weight.

<a id="proof"></a>

## Show enough evidence to help someone choose.

Add a concise service list, relevant work samples, practical policies and a credible way to verify the business. A restaurant might need a current menu and reservation route; a home service might need coverage and a quote process. Put only the proof that helps this specific decision on the page.

State changes clearly. Holiday hours, temporary closures, sold-out appointments and seasonal services can make a once-helpful page misleading when they are left in place.

<a id="search"></a>

## Keep public facts readable and check the live route.

Use visible text for the service and location instead of hiding them in an image. A specific title and description can help people recognize the page in a search result, while relevant links from your business profiles make the public address easier to find.

Open the page on a phone and complete the primary action as a new customer would. If the booking system, call link or directions fail, more search traffic will only send more people into a broken path.
