# A link-in-bio page for community groups and small nonprofits

Organize a community group's public page around its next event, volunteer task, donation route and current contact details.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/link-in-bio-for-community-groups>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

Community groups often have a small team and several urgent asks. A page can help newcomers understand the purpose, find the next activity and choose how to participate. The challenge is keeping each action current when volunteers and schedules change.

<a id="mission"></a>

## Say who the group serves and where it works.

Use a short statement of purpose with the area or audience involved. A visitor should be able to tell whether the group is local, online or both without opening several social accounts.

Link to a fuller explanation of governance and impact when it exists. Do not make unverifiable claims about outcomes or imply an affiliation the group does not have.

<a id="action"></a>

## Lead with the next useful action.

The primary route might be the next meeting, a volunteer signup or a current appeal. Put date, place, time and what someone needs to bring beside an event action. Ask for a small, understandable commitment rather than presenting every program at equal weight.

Separate participation, donations and general contact. People offering time need different information from people giving money or seeking help.

<a id="trust"></a>

## Explain where a donation link leads and who receives it.

If donations are collected elsewhere, name the destination and keep that service responsible for payment and receipts. Tell visitors what the appeal funds when the group can support that claim, and keep the explanation current.

Show a reliable contact route for questions and corrections. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal details in an informal volunteer form.

<a id="ownership"></a>

## Assign a page owner and a simple update rhythm.

Volunteers rotate, so record who changes the next event, contact address and campaign links. Before publishing a new call for help, test the form or external route from a logged-out phone.

Archive completed appeals and past meetings rather than leaving them as the first thing a newcomer sees. A modest page that someone maintains is more useful than an ambitious page that no one owns.
