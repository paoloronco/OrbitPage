# When does your public page need its own domain?

Decide when a custom domain is worth adding to a public page, and plan the move without treating a new address as an SEO shortcut.

OrbitPage · 2026-09-17 · English

[Read on OrbitPage](<https://orbitpage.com/en-US/blog/when-to-add-custom-domain>) · [Browse the archive](<https://github.com/paoloronco/OrbitPage/tree/main/docs/blog>)

A hosted address is often enough to launch. A custom domain becomes useful when people will remember, print and cite the page over time. The decision is less about looking established on day one and more about owning a stable destination you can maintain across campaigns and channels.

<a id="start"></a>

## Use the hosted address while the page's purpose is still changing.

A first release needs an accurate profile, useful actions and a working public URL more than it needs a domain purchase. Test the page with visitors before adding another operational responsibility.

A custom domain does not fix a confusing offer or thin content. Keep the first decision about the visitor journey, not the vanity of the address.

<a id="signal"></a>

## Add a domain when a consistent address matters.

A domain earns its place when the address appears on printed material, packaging, proposals, email signatures or long-lived partner references. It can also help when a team wants one recognizable brand address independent of a social network.

Check plan eligibility and decide who owns renewal and DNS access. An address is only durable when the people running the project can keep it working.

<a id="move"></a>

## Verify the new public route before replacing old references.

Connect and verify the domain in the publishing settings, then test the public page, metadata and primary links outside the editor. Update important profiles and campaign destinations after the new route works.

Keep the old address and redirects in mind when changing a widely shared URL. Broken links in printed or partner material cost more than a slightly less polished domain choice.

<a id="search"></a>

## Treat the domain as identity, not a ranking trick.

A memorable address can make the page easier to recognize and cite. Search visibility still depends on useful visible content, consistent references, crawlability and a page that answers a particular question well.

Review the canonical public URL and discovery settings after the change, but do not confuse technical correctness with an automatic increase in demand.
